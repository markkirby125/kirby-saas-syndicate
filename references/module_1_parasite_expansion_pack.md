# Module 1: Parasite Expansion Pack — Google Domain Parasites, Platform Matrix, Arbitrage & CRO

*Source: Charles Floate parasite-SEO masterclass (The Edward Show, Episode E1193, post-September 2026) & Edward Sturm Episode 1112 (`p1hExzKlHqk`). Compliance boundary for every tactic here: `../../kirby-aiseo-skill/SKILL.md` Module 15 §15.3 — if a placement's only value is borrowed host authority with no editorial mediation or standalone merit, it is on the abusive side of SRA and this skill does not build it.*

### **1.1 State of Parasite SEO (Post-Sept 2026)**
Leveraging established third-party domain authority and UGC ranking signals bypasses the fresh-site sandbox, letting pages rank same-day for competitive commercial queries and capturing AI Overview citations (all reported, single-source, unverified). Google's Site Reputation Abuse (SRA) enforcement struck **unmoderated third-party commercial leases on legacy mainstream media** (white-label directory rentals) — and paid listicle/advertorial placements on legacy outlets fall on the struck side whenever they are unmoderated commercial leases rather than editorially reviewed placements. Contextual placements, moderated UGC platforms, and developer hubs were described as remaining open (single-source, unverified). AI Overviews are reported to favor high-authority parasite platforms for commercial queries (reported, single-source, unverified) — even when the parasite URL sees organic rank volatility, LLM citation algorithms are reported to continue treating it as a consensus source.

### **1.2 Google Domain Parasites Playbook**
Ranking directly on Google-owned ecosystems, where internal domain trust is said to extend retrieval to utility content (reported, single-source, unverified):
*   **Surfaces:** Google Cloud/Firebase storage buckets and sites, Google Scripts web apps, Google Sites, YouTube video transcripts, and Google developer docs properties.
*   **Doctrine:** Publish genuinely useful content (calculators, comparison pages, documentation-style resources) on these properties for commercial queries; the host's internal trust carries retrieval **only for content with standalone utility** (reported, single-source, unverified) — thin commercial pages on Google properties face the same abuse classification as anywhere else (§1.1 boundary test). Treat as one layer of a multi-platform redundancy strategy — never a single point of failure.

### **1.3 Platform Selection Matrix — Free vs. Paid**

| Tier | Platforms | Use When |
|---|---|---|
| **Free** | LinkedIn Pulse, Reddit, Medium, GitHub, Spotify podcast transcripts, Dev.to, Substack | Budget ≤ ~$1,000; you have production capacity for standalone-value content and can wait for platform-native indexation |
| **Free (Google-domain)** | Google Cloud/Firebase, Google Scripts, Google Sites, YouTube transcripts (§1.2 — utility content only) | Query intent matches a tool/calculator/documentation resource; standalone utility is the editorial-mediation substitute |
| **Paid** | High-tier press releases, sponsored news placements, regional newswires sold with guaranteed indexing (vendor-claimed, unverified) | Budget ~$5,000; speed and the vendor-claimed indexation certainty matter; vet outlets against the newswire criteria in `../../kirby-off-page-seo/SKILL.md` Module 21 |

**Band mapping (aligned to Stage 1 intake Step 4):** $500–$5k intake ➔ free-first: free platforms + Google-domain utility assets, manual publishing, UGC-native formatting, indexation driven by internal linking between your own parasite properties; scale paid placements into the upper half of the band only for load-bearing queries. $5k–$50k intake ➔ mixed: free UGC anchors plus paid placements sold as guaranteed-indexation (vendor-claimed, unverified) acting as crawl entry points, moderated tier-2 placements at the free properties, and the aged-subreddit branch (dispatcher Stage 2) where the budget qualifies. Any paid placement MUST carry a visible, consumer-facing sponsored disclosure on the page itself, independent of platform settings, plus `rel=sponsored` where the platform marks links — paid link value without qualification is a compliance defect, not a bargain.

### **1.4 The 5-Step Production Pipeline**
1.  **Offer Selection:** High-EPC affiliate programs (SaaS, financial, crypto, lead-gen) with substantial payouts — the platform takes a conversion cut, so bounty must survive it. *(When running this pipeline under this skill, extend the Stage 1 intake with an offer/payout question — the default intake does not collect it.)*
2.  **Low-Competition SERP Spotting:** Target commercial queries whose top 10 contains forum threads, outdated blog posts, or weak niche sites.
3.  **Platform Selection:** Apply §1.3 matrix per query intent.
4.  **On-Page Intent Optimization & H1 Direct-Answer Architecture (Edward Sturm Ep. 1112, `p1hExzKlHqk`):** Exact-match title structures, high-density topic-comment opening hooks, and scannable commercial comparison tables. On Medium (which Ahrefs telemetry cited in Ep. 1112 shows at **25.7M monthly organic clicks** and **2.3M top-3 keywords**, up from 1.2M top-3 keywords in April — with **Ivan Poly's experiment** on `"AI SEO tracking tools"` ranking in the top 6 on Google and cited in both AI Overviews and AI Mode within **24 hours**; reported, single-source, unverified), apply **Tim Soulo's (Ahrefs CMO) H1 Direct-Answer Pattern** (only under the verified, disclosed byline of §1.6): **`"Peec AI alternatives: Why I Think Ahrefs Brand Radar is the Better Choice"`** *(ranked #3 on Google for the target keyword, single-source and unverified; transcribed phonetically as `"Peak AI"` in `p1hExzKlHqk`)*. Placing `[Competitor] Alternatives: Why I Think [Your Brand] Is the Better Choice` directly in the `H1` / Title Tag auto-populates the Medium URL slug with both entities and front-loads the recommendation to align with reported LLM positional weighting on the **beginning and end** of retrieved documents (directional, single-source, unverified) (`../../kirby-off-page-seo/SKILL.md` Module 19 §19.2D).
5.  **Canonical Management & Indexation:** Detect platform-level canonical bugs (self-referential or parent-category canonicals) and route to the canonical-audit SOP in `../../kirby-off-page-seo/SKILL.md` Module 21 — remediate via platform-native fixes or shift budget off the platform; do not attempt to 'bypass' a canonical you do not control. Drive indexation through internal linking among your own properties (§1.3 band mapping) and moderated placements (§1.5 step 2); no mass non-editorial tier-2 link blasts (the SRA pattern of §1.1).

### **1.5 Internal Link Arbitrage SOP**
Buying or building **internal links within the parasite host domain** often outperforms expensive tier-1 external backlink campaigns (directional, single-source, unverified):
1.  Map the host domain's own pages that already rank adjacent to your target query.
2.  Acquire links from those pages to your parasite page via platform-native, moderated means (comments where moderated, related-article placements); self-serve profile/category links only where platform ToS allows and the placement survives editorial review — mass non-editorial self-placement is the SRA pattern of §1.1. Any paid link still carries the §1.3 sponsored-disclosure duty.
3.  This transfers the host's own page-level relevance, not just domain authority — cheaper and more durable than external equivalents (directional, single-source, unverified).

### **1.6 Parasite CRO Framework (The Conversion Dilemma)**
Parasite pages are reported to convert below native brand sites (directional, single-source, unverified) due to platform framing. Required stack:
*   **Embedded comparison widgets** (interactive, not screenshots) so the commercial value is consumed on-page.
*   **Explicit visual CTA blocks** with a single dominant action.
*   **Clear disclaimers** (affiliate relationship, FTC) — trust deficit is the conversion killer; disclosure raises it (directional, single-source, unverified).
*   **High-trust bridge funnels:** link to a neutral comparison/brand page the user can verify, rather than demanding the conversion in-platform.
*   **The `@username` Prefix De-Indexing Risk & FTC 16 CFR Part 255 Guardrail (Edward Sturm Ep. 1112, `p1hExzKlHqk`):**
    *   *Pogo-Sticking & Prefix De-Indexing:* Because Medium and LinkedIn structure author content under `medium.com/@username/article-slug` and `linkedin.com/in/username`, overly salesy copy that triggers user **pogo-sticking** (clicking the result, bouncing back to the SERP, and selecting another result) makes it very easy for Google to **de-index everything after that `@username` prefix** (single podcast source, unverified). The remedy is genuinely useful, clearly disclosed content, not softened copy written to avoid a detector.
    *   *No Undisclosed Employee-as-Consultant Personas:* While Ep. 1112 (`p1hExzKlHqk`) describes an employee presenting their Medium profile as an unbranded "consultant" rather than an employee of the company to recommend the brand, operating undisclosed employee or fabricated consultant personas violates **FTC 16 CFR Part 255** (endorsement disclosure rules), the `§1.1` SRA boundary test, and `../../kirby-off-page-seo/SKILL.md` Module 4 §4.3 (*Profile Stealth*: no fabricated personas) and §4.6 (inauthentic citations). Execute Medium and social comparison assets **only** via: (1) a **transparent, verified executive/founder profile** (the Tim Soulo CMO model: *"Why I Think [Our Brand] Is the Better Choice"*), or (2) **sponsor-disclosed practitioner partnerships** (`#Sponsored` / FTC disclosure) so the asset's value does not rest on links back to the brand alone, diversified across **Medium, LinkedIn, Threads, X, and Facebook** (`../../kirby-off-page-seo/SKILL.md` Module 19 §19.2D).
*   **Byline & Common-Ownership Rule:** Every byline on a parasite or comparison asset names a real, verifiable author. No AI-generated headshots, no synthetic or fabricated personas, no invented credentials (`../../kirby-off-page-seo/SKILL.md` Module 4 §4.3). Any listicle, comparison or alternatives page, review, video or podcast asset that features two or more brands the user owns or shares ownership of MUST disclose the common ownership, visibly and in plain words, near the top of the page (on screen and spoken, for video or podcast assets); this extends the sponsor-disclosure rule in the persona and sponsor bullet above. Whether Google also treats undisclosed common ownership as a spam signal is unverified; the FTC disclosure duty applies regardless.
